What is an MRI Scan?

Magnetic Resonance Imaging (MRI) is a safe, non-invasive imaging technique that uses a powerful magnetic field and radio waves to produce highly detailed images of organs, soft tissues, joints, muscles, nerves, and blood vessels. Unlike CT scans or X-rays, MRI does not use ionizing radiation, making it an excellent imaging modality for many neurological, musculoskeletal, abdominal, pelvic, and vascular conditions.

Why Choose a 3 Tesla MRI?

Our advanced 3 Tesla MRI system provides:

Superior image quality and resolution

Greater anatomical detail

Enhanced visualization of nerves, ligaments, cartilage, and soft tissues

Faster scan protocols for selected examinations

Improved detection of subtle abnormalities

Advanced imaging for neurological, musculoskeletal, vascular, and oncological conditions

The higher field strength of a 3 Tesla MRI allows for greater diagnostic confidence, particularly in complex clinical cases.

What is a CT Scan?

A Computed Tomography (CT) Scan is an advanced imaging technique that uses X-rays and sophisticated computer processing to create detailed cross-sectional images of the body's internal organs, bones, blood vessels, and soft tissues.

CT scans are widely used because they are fast, accurate, and highly effective in diagnosing a variety of medical conditions, particularly in emergency situations.

Why Choose CT Imaging?

CT scanning offers several important advantages:

Fast image acquisition

High-resolution cross-sectional imaging

Excellent evaluation of bones, lungs, abdomen and blood vessels

Advanced 3D image reconstruction

Accurate diagnosis for emergency and routine conditions

What is an OPG?

An Orthopantomogram (OPG) is a panoramic digital X-ray that provides a single comprehensive image of:

Upper and lower jaws

All teeth

Temporomandibular joints (TMJ)

Maxillary sinuses

Jaw bones

Surrounding structures

It is one of the most commonly performed dental imaging examinations and assists dentists in evaluating overall oral health.

What is CBCT?

Cone Beam Computed Tomography (CBCT) is an advanced three-dimensional imaging technique that provides highly detailed images of the teeth, jawbones, facial bones, airways, and surrounding structures.

Unlike conventional dental X-rays, CBCT produces 3D images that allow precise assessment of complex dental and maxillofacial conditions, making it invaluable for diagnosis and treatment planning.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the difference between an OPG and a CBCT?

An OPG provides a two-dimensional panoramic image of your teeth and jaws, making it suitable for routine dental evaluation.

A CBCT provides detailed three-dimensional images and is typically recommended for complex dental procedures such as implants, impacted teeth, orthodontic planning, and oral surgery.

Is CBCT safe?

Yes. CBCT uses X-rays and is performed only when clinically indicated. Our equipment follows radiation safety guidelines and uses optimized exposure protocols while maintaining excellent image quality.

Do I need an appointment?

Appointments are recommended for CBCT examinations. OPG imaging may also be available for walk-in patients, subject to availability.

Will I receive the images?

Yes. Images and reports can be provided in the format requested by your treating dentist or specialist.

Can children undergo CBCT?

Yes. CBCT may be performed in children when clinically indicated, with protocols optimized to minimize radiation exposure.

What is Fetal Medicine?

Fetal Medicine is a specialized field focused on the health, growth, and development of the unborn baby throughout pregnancy. It involves advanced ultrasound examinations that help monitor fetal development, identify congenital abnormalities, assess placental function, evaluate fetal well-being, and guide the management of both routine and high-risk pregnancies.

Regular fetal assessments allow early detection of potential concerns, enabling timely intervention and informed pregnancy care.

Our Fetal Medicine Services

Early Pregnancy Scan

Confirmation of pregnancy, assessment of fetal viability, dating of pregnancy, and evaluation of early pregnancy health. Also called as viability scan or dating scan.

Nuchal Translucency (NT) Scan

Performed between 11 weeks and 13 weeks 6 days, the NT scan evaluates the risk of chromosomal abnormalities while also assessing early fetal anatomy.

Anomaly Scan

A detailed examination performed between 18 and 22 weeks to evaluate fetal anatomy, growth, and structural development.

Growth Scan

Assessment of fetal growth, estimated fetal weight, amniotic fluid, placental position, and overall fetal well-being during the second and third trimesters.

Fetal Doppler Study

Evaluation of blood flow in the umbilical cord, fetal circulation, uterine arteries, and other vessels to monitor fetal well-being, especially in high-risk pregnancies.

Cervical Length Assessment

Evaluation of cervical length to assess the risk of preterm birth in selected pregnancies.

Multiple Pregnancy Monitoring

Specialized ultrasound assessment for twin and higher-order pregnancies with close monitoring of fetal growth and well-being.

High-Risk Pregnancy Imaging

Comprehensive fetal assessment for pregnancies complicated by maternal medical conditions, fetal abnormalities, growth restriction, hypertension, diabetes, previous pregnancy complications, or other high-risk factors.

Fetal Echocardiography

Detailed evaluation of the fetal heart when clinically indicated or recommended by the treating obstetrician.

Fetal MRI

Advanced fetal imaging in selected clinical situations when additional information beyond ultrasound is required.

What is an Early Pregnancy Scan?

An Early Pregnancy Scan, also known as a Viability Scan, is an ultrasound examination performed during the first trimester to evaluate the early development of your pregnancy.

The scan helps confirm that the pregnancy is developing normally and provides valuable information for both you and your obstetrician.

It is often the first opportunity to visualize your baby and, depending on the stage of pregnancy, detect the fetal heartbeat.

Why is an Early Pregnancy Scan Performed?

An early pregnancy scan helps to:

Confirm an intrauterine pregnancy

Assess fetal viability

Detect the fetal heartbeat

Determine gestational age

Estimate the expected date of delivery (EDD)

Confirm the number of fetuses

Evaluate the gestational sac and yolk sac

Assess early fetal development

Exclude ectopic pregnancy when clinically indicated

Identify early pregnancy complications if present

When Should an Early Pregnancy Scan Be Done?

An early pregnancy scan is generally performed between 6 and 9 weeks of pregnancy, although the timing may vary depending on your doctor's recommendation and your clinical situation.

Your obstetrician may advise an earlier or later scan based on symptoms, previous pregnancy history, fertility treatment, or other medical considerations.

Gestational Sac

Yolk Sac

Embryo (Fetal Pole)

Fetal Heartbeat

Crown-Rump Length (CRL)

Number of Fetuses

Pregnancy Location

Uterus and Adnexa

As pregnancy progresses, additional developmental milestones become visible.

Who Should Consider an Early Pregnancy Scan?

Have a positive pregnancy test

Wish to confirm your pregnancy

Have abdominal pain or bleeding

Have a history of miscarriage

Have undergone fertility treatment

Have irregular menstrual cycles

Have had a previous ectopic pregnancy

Are carrying twins or multiple pregnancies

NT Scan (Nuchal Translucency) & Nasal Bone Assessment

First Trimester Screening for a Healthy Pregnancy

What is an NT Scan?

The Nuchal Translucency (NT) Scan is a specialized first-trimester ultrasound performed between 11 weeks and 13 weeks 6 days of pregnancy.

The examination measures the fluid-filled space behind the baby's neck (nuchal translucency) and evaluates several important fetal markers, including the presence of the nasal bone, early fetal anatomy, and overall fetal development.

The NT scan forms an important part of first trimester screening for chromosomal abnormalities and structural abnormalities when combined with maternal age, medical history, and, where recommended, blood tests.

Why is the NT Scan Important?

The NT scan helps to:

Confirm fetal viability

Assess fetal growth

Measure nuchal translucency

Assess the nasal bone

Evaluate early fetal anatomy

Confirm gestational age

Determine the number of fetuses

Identify certain structural abnormalities that may be detectable in early pregnancy

Assess the risk of certain chromosomal abnormalities as part of combined first trimester screening

It is important to understand that the NT scan is a screening test, not a diagnostic test. Your obstetrician will interpret the findings along with your medical history and any recommended blood tests.

When Should the NT Scan Be Performed?

The NT scan should be performed between:

11 Weeks and 13 Weeks + 6 Days

or when the baby's Crown-Rump Length (CRL) measures approximately 45–84 mm.

This is the recommended period because the measurements are most accurate during this stage of pregnancy.

What is Evaluated During the Scan?

During the examination, the radiologist assesses:

Fetal Heartbeat

Crown-Rump Length (CRL)

Nuchal Translucency Measurement

Nasal Bone

Skull and Brain

Spine

Face

Limbs

Stomach

Urinary Bladder

Abdominal Wall

Placental Location

Amniotic Fluid

Number of Fetuses

Chorionicity in Twin Pregnancies (when applicable)

Who Should Have an NT Scan?

The NT scan is recommended for all pregnant women, regardless of age or risk factors, as part of routine first trimester prenatal care.

It is particularly important for women who:

Are above 35 years of age

Have a family history of genetic disorders

Have had a previous pregnancy with chromosomal abnormalities

Have conceived through assisted reproductive techniques

Have abnormal blood test results

Are carrying twins or multiple pregnancies

Have been advised by their obstetrician to undergo first trimester screening

Combined First Trimester Screening

The NT scan is often combined with maternal blood tests to improve the assessment of the risk of certain chromosomal abnormalities.

Your obstetrician will advise whether combined screening or additional tests, such as Non-Invasive Prenatal Testing (NIPT), are appropriate based on your individual clinical circumstances.

Yes. The NT scan uses ultrasound technology and does not involve radiation. It is considered safe for both mother and baby when performed by qualified healthcare professionals.

Can the NT scan detect Down syndrome?

The NT scan is a screening examination that helps estimate the risk of certain chromosomal abnormalities, including Down syndrome. It does not confirm or exclude these conditions. Further testing may be recommended if the screening indicates an increased risk.

Is the nasal bone assessment performed during the same scan?

Yes. In most pregnancies, the nasal bone is assessed during the NT scan as part of the first trimester fetal evaluation.

What happens if the NT measurement is increased?

An increased NT measurement does not necessarily mean that the baby has a problem. Your obstetrician may recommend additional investigations, such as NIPT, detailed ultrasound, or other diagnostic tests, depending on the overall clinical assessment.

What is an Early Anomaly Scan?

An Early Anomaly Scan is a detailed ultrasound examination performed during the early second trimester, usually between 16 and 18 weeks of pregnancy, to evaluate the baby's developing organs and anatomy.

Unlike the NT scan, which primarily focuses on screening for chromosomal abnormalities and early fetal markers, the Early Anomaly Scan provides a more comprehensive assessment of fetal structural development. While not a replacement for the detailed second trimester anomaly scan, it can detect many significant abnormalities at an earlier stage.

Why is an Early Anomaly Scan Important?

The Early Anomaly Scan helps to:

Evaluate early fetal anatomy in detail

Assess fetal growth and development

Examine the developing brain and skull

Evaluate the spine

Assess the face and facial profile

Examine the heart and major blood vessels

Evaluate the stomach, kidneys, bladder, and abdominal wall

Assess the upper and lower limbs

Identify certain major structural abnormalities that may be visible during the first trimester

Support early pregnancy counselling and management when required

Early diagnosis provides more time for further evaluation, counselling, and pregnancy planning if additional investigations become necessary.

When Should the Scan Be Performed?

The Early Anomaly Scan is generally recommended between:

16 and 18 weeks of pregnancy

The exact timing may vary depending on your obstetrician's recommendation and individual clinical circumstances.

Who Should Consider an Early Anomaly Scan?

You wish to have an early detailed assessment of your baby's development

There is a family history of congenital abnormalities

You have a high-risk pregnancy

You conceived through assisted reproductive techniques

You have diabetes or other maternal medical conditions

Your obstetrician recommends additional fetal assessment

Many fetal medicine specialists now recommend an Early Anomaly Scan as part of comprehensive prenatal care, particularly for pregnancies requiring closer monitoring.

Does This Replace the 18–22 Week Anomaly Scan?

No.

The Early Anomaly Scan complements—but does not replace—the detailed second trimester anomaly scan performed between 18 and 22 weeks.

While many structural abnormalities can be detected early, some organs continue to develop throughout pregnancy and are best assessed during the mid-trimester anomaly scan. Both examinations together provide a more comprehensive evaluation of fetal development.

What is an Anomaly Scan?

It is designed to systematically examine your baby's anatomy from head to toe, helping identify structural abnormalities, monitor normal development, and provide reassurance regarding your baby's growth.

The anomaly scan is considered one of the most important routine ultrasound examinations during pregnancy.

Why is the Anomaly Scan Important?

Evaluate your baby's anatomy in detail

Assess normal fetal growth and development

Identify many major structural abnormalities

Assess placental location and appearance

Measure amniotic fluid volume

Evaluate fetal movements

Assess fetal well-being

Assist your obstetrician in planning ongoing pregnancy care

Although no ultrasound can detect every congenital abnormality, the anomaly scan significantly improves the detection of many major structural conditions.

18 and 22 weeks of pregnancy

What is Fetal Echocardiography?

Fetal Echocardiography, commonly known as Fetal Echo, is a specialized ultrasound examination that provides a detailed evaluation of your baby's heart while still in the womb.

Unlike a routine pregnancy ultrasound, Fetal Echo focuses exclusively on the fetal heart, assessing its anatomy, chambers, valves, blood vessels, blood flow, rhythm, and function.

The examination helps identify many congenital heart defects before birth, allowing appropriate counselling, monitoring, and delivery planning when necessary.

Why is Fetal Echocardiography Important?

Congenital heart disease is one of the most common birth defects.

Early diagnosis can:

Detect many congenital heart abnormalities before birth

Evaluate fetal heart structure and function

Assess fetal heart rhythm

Monitor pregnancies at increased risk of congenital heart disease

Help obstetricians and pediatric cardiologists plan appropriate care

Facilitate delivery planning at suitable healthcare facilities when required

Provide reassurance when the fetal heart is developing normally

When is Fetal Echo Performed?

18 and 24 weeks of pregnancy

What is a Fetal Doppler Scan?

A Fetal Doppler Scan is a specialized ultrasound examination that evaluates the blood flow between the placenta and your baby. Unlike a routine growth scan, Doppler imaging assesses how efficiently oxygen and nutrients are being delivered to the developing fetus.

By studying blood flow patterns in specific fetal and maternal blood vessels, Doppler studies help assess placental function, fetal circulation, and overall fetal well-being.

Why is a Fetal Doppler Scan Important?

Assess placental function

Monitor fetal well-being

Evaluate fetal circulation

Detect signs of fetal compromise

What is a Cervical Length Scan?

A Cervical Length Scan is a specialized ultrasound examination used to measure the length of the cervix during pregnancy.

The cervix normally remains long and closed throughout most of pregnancy. A shortened cervix may increase the risk of preterm labour and premature birth. Measuring cervical length helps your obstetrician identify women who may benefit from closer monitoring or preventive treatment.

The examination is most commonly performed using transvaginal ultrasound (TVS), which provides the most accurate and reliable assessment.

Why is a Cervical Length Scan Important?

A Cervical Length Scan helps to:

Assess the risk of preterm birth

Measure cervical length accurately

Detect cervical shortening

Identify cervical funneling, if present

Monitor cervical changes during pregnancy

Guide decisions regarding preventive treatment

Support timely pregnancy management

Provide reassurance when cervical findings are normal

Early identification of cervical shortening allows your obstetrician to recommend appropriate management aimed at reducing the risk of preterm delivery.

When is the Scan Performed?

A Cervical Length Scan is commonly performed between:

16 and 24 weeks of pregnancy

Your obstetrician may recommend additional examinations later in pregnancy if continued monitoring is required.

Who Should Have a Cervical Length Scan?

Have a previous preterm birth

Have a history of second-trimester pregnancy loss

Are carrying twins or multiple pregnancies

Have undergone previous cervical surgery

Have suspected cervical insufficiency

Experience pelvic pressure or uterine contractions

Have vaginal bleeding during pregnancy

Have an ultrasound suggesting cervical shortening

How is the Scan Performed?

The examination is usually performed using a transvaginal ultrasound (TVS).

A specially designed ultrasound probe is gently placed into the vagina to obtain clear images of the cervix. This method provides the most accurate measurement while remaining safe for both mother and baby.

The procedure is generally comfortable, takes only a few minutes, and does not increase the risk of miscarriage or preterm labour.

What is a Doppler Study?

A Doppler study is a specialized ultrasound examination that evaluates the direction, speed, and pattern of blood flow within arteries and veins. Unlike a routine ultrasound, Doppler imaging provides valuable information about circulation, helping diagnose narrowing of blood vessels, blockages, blood clots, varicose veins, vascular insufficiency, and many other circulatory disorders.

Since Doppler uses ultrasound technology, it is safe, non-invasive, painless, and free from ionizing radiation.

What is a 2D Echo?

A 2D Echocardiography (2D Echo) is an ultrasound examination of the heart that uses high-frequency sound waves to create real-time images of the heart's chambers, valves, muscles, and major blood vessels.

Unlike CT scans or X-rays, a 2D Echo does not use radiation. It is a safe, painless, and non-invasive investigation that provides valuable information about how well your heart is functioning.

Why is a 2D Echo Performed?

A 2D Echo helps your doctor:

Evaluate heart function

Assess heart muscle movement

Examine heart valves

Detect congenital heart disease

Evaluate heart enlargement

Assess pumping efficiency (Ejection Fraction)

Detect fluid around the heart (Pericardial Effusion)

Evaluate cardiomyopathy

Assess pulmonary hypertension

Monitor known heart disease

Guide treatment decisions

Radiology Second Opinion

Expert Review of Your MRI, CT, Ultrasound & X-Ray

At Clarus Magnus Health & Diagnostics, we understand that important medical decisions often depend on accurate interpretation of imaging studies. Our Radiology Second Opinion Service provides an independent expert review of MRI, CT scans, Ultrasound, X-rays, Mammography, PET-CT, and other imaging examinations performed elsewhere.

Whether you have received a new diagnosis, have persistent symptoms despite treatment, are considering surgery, or simply want additional reassurance, our experienced radiologists provide a thorough, unbiased review of your imaging to help you and your treating doctor make informed decisions.

With expertise across multiple subspecialties including Musculoskeletal Imaging, Neuroimaging, Body Imaging, Fetal Medicine, Women's Imaging, and Emergency Radiology, we are committed to delivering accurate, clinically relevant, and evidence-based interpretations.

What is a Radiology Second Opinion?

A radiology second opinion involves an independent review of your imaging studies by an experienced radiologist.

The review includes:

Careful reassessment of all images

Review of the original report (when available)

Correlation with your clinical history

Identification of additional or overlooked findings where applicable

Preparation of a comprehensive second opinion report

A second opinion does not necessarily mean the original report is incorrect. It provides an additional expert perspective that may help guide diagnosis and treatment.

When Should You Consider a Second Opinion?

A second opinion may be valuable if:

You have been advised to undergo surgery.

Your symptoms do not match your imaging report.

You have persistent pain despite treatment.

The diagnosis is uncertain or complex.

You would like confirmation before making an important treatment decision.

Your doctor recommends an expert review.

You are seeking consultation with another specialist.

Your scan was performed at another diagnostic centre.

You have a sports injury requiring detailed assessment.

You have a neurological disorder requiring specialized interpretation.

You have a complex cancer diagnosis requiring multidisciplinary care.
